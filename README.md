## Introduction

Public administration is the science and art of translating democratic decisions, statutory mandates, and public policy into functioning public services. Where political science asks how power is contested and won, public administration investigates how power is operationalized: how government agencies manage public funds, structure civil services, design regulatory rules, deliver frontline services, and generate public value while remaining accountable to constitutional law and the public interest.

This curriculum is built for independent self-directed learners seeking a rigorous, practical grounding in the public and nonprofit sectors. It assumes no previous background in political science, economics, or administrative law. Every foundational concept — from market failure economics to civil service merit systems — is introduced from first principles using open-access textbooks, university open courseware, practical field manuals, and canonical monographs.

### How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum is structured into four interconnected thematic clusters that move from external institutional context to internal operations, policy execution, and constitutional oversight:

- **Institutional and Conceptual Foundations:** Start with *Introduction to Public Administration*, *Public Administration Theory*, *Government and Political Institutions*, and *Economics for the Public Sector*. These four subjects establish the intellectual evolution of the discipline, the separation-of-powers environment that constrains agencies, and the economic rationale for government intervention (such as public goods and market failures).
- **Internal Operations and Management:** Next, examine how public agencies function from the inside. Study *Public Organization Theory*, *Public Management, Strategic Leadership, and Public Value*, *Public Human Resource Management*, and *Public Budgeting and Financial Management*. This sequence covers organizational dynamics, executive decision-making, civil service merit protections, and the twin engines of government finance: political budget allocation and applied fiscal administration.
- **The Policy Cycle and Frontline Delivery:** Turn to *Public Policy Process*, *Policy Analysis and Program Evaluation*, and *Policy Implementation and Street-Level Bureaucracy*. This group traces policies from agenda setting and problem definition through criteria-based decision analysis to frontline delivery, where caseworkers, inspectors, and other street-level bureaucrats exercise administrative discretion.
- **Democratic Accountability and Legal Limits:** Conclude with *Administrative Law and Regulatory Procedure* and *Ethics, Integrity, and Public Accountability*. These subjects examine the procedural boundaries of delegated administrative power (such as notice-and-comment rulemaking, due process, and judicial review) along with the ethical frameworks and oversight mechanisms necessary to combat corruption and protect public trust.

While the institutional foundations should be completed first, the management and policy clusters can be studied either sequentially or concurrently depending on your specific learning goals.

### Scope

This curriculum focuses strictly on the mandatory undergraduate core of the discipline — the shared body of institutional, financial, organizational, and legal knowledge that every practitioner and analyst needs. It deliberately leaves out specialized econometric research methods, advanced quantitative modeling, and narrow subfields. 

Once you have established this foundational core, you can branch out into specialized areas through the companion guides in this series:

- Explore advanced subdisciplines — such as municipal project finance, algorithmic governance, comparative development administration, and nonprofit board governance — in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).
- Deepen your historical and theoretical understanding through landmark primary texts and essays in [Readings](extras/readings.md).
- Engage with complete recorded university lectures and specialized masterclasses in [Courses](extras/courses.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Public Administration

Surveys the foundational scope, constitutional origins, historical evolution, and core operating principles of public sector administration.

[Public Administration: The Essentials (Jayme L. Renfro)](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/facbook/1637/) - The primary open-access introductory textbook, providing a free, accessible overview of the history, institutional setting, and everyday practice of public administration.

[Introducing Public Administration (Jay M. Shafritz et al.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781032209340) - A comprehensive commercial textbook alternative to Renfro, offering deeper coverage of administrative history, intergovernmental relations, and administrative culture.

[Public Administration: Concepts and Cases (Richard J. Stillman II)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780618993093) - A case-oriented alternative that teaches core concepts through classic essays paired with historical public sector case studies, ideal for inductive learners.

### Public Administration Theory

Traces the intellectual paradigms of the discipline from classical bureaucracy and scientific management to New Public Management and public value governance.

[Classics of Public Administration (Jay M. Shafritz & Albert C. Hyde, Eds.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305639034) - The canonical reader compiling foundational primary source essays from Woodrow Wilson, Max Weber, Chester Barnard, Herbert Simon, and Dwight Waldo.

### Government and Political Institutions

Examines the constitutional rules, legislative oversight mechanisms, and intergovernmental dynamics that structure administrative power.

[American Government 3e (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/american-government-3e) - The core open-access textbook for understanding the constitutional bureaucracy, separation of powers, and federalism within a presidential administrative system.

[Introduction to Political Science (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/introduction-political-science) - An alternative open-access text for international learners seeking comparative regimes, state capacity, and institutional structures outside the United States model.

[Making Public Policy (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/11-002j-making-public-policy-fall-2014/) - A complementary course pack that provides syllabus readings and lecture notes exploring how legislative coalitions and interest groups constrain administrative agencies.

### Economics for the Public Sector

Analyzes market failures, externalities, public goods provision, and the economic rationale for government intervention and regulatory policy.

[Public Finance and Public Policy (MIT OpenCourseWare/Jonathan Gruber)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-41-public-finance-and-public-policy-fall-2024/) - The primary multimedia course for this subject, offering complete university video lectures and problem sets on externalities, social insurance, and cost-benefit principles.

[Economics of the Public Sector (Joseph E. Stiglitz & Jay K. Rosengard)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780393925227) - A comprehensive textbook alternative to Gruber's course for learners who prefer a systematic, print-based treatment of welfare economics, efficiency, and taxation.

[The Economy (CORE-Econ)](https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/) - An open-access companion reader that introduces economic governance, collective action, and market failures from a broader societal perspective.

### Public Organization Theory

Explores organizational behavior, bureaucratic structure, informal networks, and systemic change within public sector agencies.

[Understanding and Managing Public Organizations (Hal G. Rainey, Sergio Fernandez, & Deanna Malatesta)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781119705895) - The definitive core textbook analyzing the unique operating environments, goal ambiguity, public service motivation, and bureaucratic structures of public agencies.

[Organizational Behavior (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/organizational-behavior) - An optional open-access supplement that covers baseline workplace psychology, group dynamics, and organizational design for learners who want foundational business-school concepts before focusing on government.

### Public Management, Strategic Leadership, and Public Value

Examines executive leadership models, administrative competencies, and strategic frameworks for creating public value within public and non-profit agencies.

[Leadership for Purposeful Change: Creating Public Value (Harvard University/Mark Moore)](https://pll.harvard.edu/course/leadership-purposeful-change-creating-public-value) - A free-to-audit online course where Mark Moore introduces the strategic triangle framework for aligning authorizing environments, operational capability, and public value creation.

[Creating Public Value: Strategic Management in Government (Mark H. Moore)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674175587) - The canonical monograph on strategic management in government, serving as the in-depth textual companion to Moore's online course.

[Leadership in Public Organizations: An Introduction (Montgomery Van Wart)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780765647023) - A complementary guide focusing on the operational leadership styles, administrative competencies, and change management tools needed inside public agencies.

### Public Human Resource Management

Covers civil service merit systems, public-sector recruitment, workplace diversity, employee motivation, and collective bargaining.

[Human Resource Management in Public Service: Paradoxes, Processes, and Problems (Evan M. Berman et al.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071848906) - The primary narrative textbook covering civil service history, merit protections, position classification, compensation, and public-sector labor relations.

[Public Personnel Management: Current Concerns, Future Challenges (Norma M. Riccucci, Ed.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781032516677) - A complementary reader exploring contemporary controversies and legal debates, including civil service reform, affirmative action, and public pensions.

### Public Budgeting and Financial Management

Examines the politics of the budgetary cycle, resource allocation conflicts, governmental accounting, revenue systems, and fiscal administration.

[Financial Strategy for Public Managers (Sharon Kioko & Justin Marlowe)](https://uw.pressbooks.pub/financialstrategy/) - The primary open-access technical textbook, providing applied instruction on governmental accounting, financial statement analysis, and cost modeling.

[The Politics of Public Budgeting: Getting and Spending, Borrowing and Balancing (Irene S. Rubin)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781544325057) - A complementary core text focusing on the political side of fiscal decision-making, executive-legislative negotiations, and spending trade-offs.

[Public Financial Management (IMFx/edX)](https://www.edx.org/learn/finance/international-monetary-fund-public-financial-management) - An interactive online course alternative covering the entire public financial management cycle, including budget preparation, treasury execution, and fiscal risk.

[Fiscal Administration: Analysis and Applications for the Public Sector (John L. Mikesell)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305953680) - An exhaustive commercial reference text alternative for learners seeking comprehensive technical detail on tax structures, user charges, and municipal bond issuance.

### Public Policy Process

Surveys how policy issues emerge, reach institutional agendas, undergo legislative formulation, and achieve political adoption.

[An Introduction to the Policy Process (Thomas A. Birkland)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138495548) - The core textbook explaining agenda setting, policy subsystems, advocacy coalitions, policy instruments, and the stages of policy formulation.

[Using Public Policy for Social Change (Coursera/University of Michigan)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/using-public-policy-for-social-change) - A free-to-audit online course alternative or companion that explores policy cycles with an emphasis on citizen advocacy and community-level change.

### Policy Analysis and Program Evaluation

Introduces criteria-based decision analysis, cost-benefit reasoning, and systematic methodologies for evaluating public program outcomes.

[A Practical Guide for Policy Analysis: The Eightfold Path to More Effective Problem Solving (Eugene Bardach & Eric M. Patashnik)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071884133) - The most accessible, practitioner-oriented primer on defining policy problems, assembling evaluative criteria, projecting outcomes, and confronting trade-offs.

[Policy Analysis: Concepts and Practice (David L. Weimer & Aidan R. Vining)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138216471) - A comprehensive academic textbook alternative to Bardach, offering formal microeconomic grounding, criteria-alternative matrices, and cost-benefit modeling.

[Introduction to Program Evaluation for Public Health Programs: A Self-Study Guide (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/26245) - A free, open-access field guide focusing specifically on the evaluation stage, teaching logic models, stakeholder engagement, and indicator design.

### Policy Implementation and Street-Level Bureaucracy

Examines frontline public service delivery, administrative discretion, rule enforcement dilemmas, and common causes of implementation breakdown.

[Street-Level Bureaucracy: Dilemmas of the Individual in Public Services (Michael Lipsky)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780871545442) - The seminal treatise detailing how frontline workers, including police officers, teachers, and caseworkers, exercise daily discretion to effectively shape public policy.

### Administrative Law and Regulatory Procedure

Governs statutory delegations of power, notice-and-comment rulemaking, administrative adjudication, due process, and judicial review of agency actions.

[Administrative Law [No. 86] (The Federalist Society/YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWwcngsYgoUXTBxtM1cr9ThwGsTTR4XU_) - A structured video lecture series introducing constitutional nondelegation, administrative rulemaking, adjudication, and judicial deference doctrines.

[Examples & Explanations: Administrative Law (William F. Funk & Richard H. Seamon)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9798892073592) - A complementary problem-oriented study guide that uses concrete scenarios and explanations to test comprehension of administrative law principles.

### Ethics, Integrity, and Public Accountability

Addresses conflicts of interest, moral decision-making frameworks, whistleblower protections, anti-corruption systems, and democratic oversight mechanisms.

[The Ethics Challenge in Public Service: A Problem-Solving Guide (Carol W. Lewis & Stuart C. Gilman)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781118109861) - The foundational practitioner guide to recognizing and resolving ethical dilemmas, conflicts of interest, and integrity challenges in public office.

[Ethics in Public Policy (Coursera/O.P. Jindal Global University)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ethics-in-public-policy) - A free-to-audit online course alternative covering moral philosophy, rights-based versus utilitarian trade-offs, and public accountability systems.
